Where Conservation Meets Innovation: Srinivasulu, IFS

Outlook India's Celebrating the Nation Builders series profiles a rare administrator who has married science with sensitivity, technology with trust, and conservation with community participation.

Celebrating the Nation Builders · January 1, 2026

In an era when environmental governance is often caught between urgency and complexity, Srinivasulu of the Indian Forest Service stands out as a rare administrator who has managed to marry science with sensitivity, technology with trust, and conservation with community participation. Over more than two decades in public service, his career has quietly redefined what modern environmental leadership in India can look like.

Academic Foundation
Gold Medalist in Agriculture, UAS Dharwad. Top national performer in molecular biology & biotechnology.
Kali Tiger Reserve
500+ families brought into mainstream. Thousands of hectares of inviolate space created.
Digital Innovation
Mobile pollution monitoring, real-time effluent tracking, and e-governance dashboards at KSPCB.
Guinness Recognition
Turmeric Ganesha campaign — largest online photo album of handmade sculpture (2022).

Science-Led Administration

Trained first as a scientist, Srinivasulu's approach to conservation was shaped long before he entered the civil services. His academic rigour became the backbone of his administrative philosophy — decisions guided by data, policies informed by evidence, and innovation grounded in ecological realities.

Conservation Through Community

As Field Director of the Kali Tiger Reserve, he moved beyond the traditional boundaries of wildlife protection, demonstrating that the real challenge lies not within the forest but in the villages around it. He showed that conservation succeeds not when wildlife is protected from people, but when communities are empowered through green livelihoods.

Digital Governance at KSPCB

Innovation became even more visible during his tenure as Member Secretary of the Karnataka State Pollution Control Board. At a time when environmental regulation was often criticised for opacity, he introduced a suite of digital tools that changed how regulators, industries, and citizens interacted — from mobile applications for pollution monitoring and real-time effluent vehicle tracking to COVID-19 biomedical waste monitoring platforms and e-governance dashboards.

Storytelling as a Conservation Tool

Beyond administration and technology, his impact extends into storytelling as a powerful tool for conservation. He conceptualised and produced the award-winning documentary The Kali, which helped shape public perception and contributed to the renaming of the tiger reserve itself.
