Turning Waste into Wealth: Karnataka's ₹20,000-Crore Circular Economy Opportunity

Karnataka's waste streams — municipal solid waste, e-waste, construction debris, sewage — represent an untapped circular economy worth ₹15,000–20,000 crore annually and 1–2 lakh new jobs.

S
Srinivasulu IFS
Principal Secretary (Ecology & Environment) & Director General, EMPRI · Government of Karnataka

Karnataka stands at a critical crossroads. Rapid urbanisation, industrial growth, and rising consumption are generating unprecedented volumes of waste across the state. What is widely viewed as an environmental burden, however, holds the potential to become one of Karnataka's strongest economic drivers.

If fully harnessed, Karnataka's waste economy could unlock a circular economy worth ₹15,000–20,000 crore annually while generating 1–2 lakh jobs and reducing emissions by up to 25%.

Municipal Solid Waste

Urban Karnataka generates nearly 13,000 tonnes of municipal solid waste (MSW) every day, with Bengaluru alone contributing about 6,000 tonnes. Around 50–60% is organic, 15–25% is recyclable dry waste, and 20–30% is inert material, including construction debris and silt. Efficient waste management can produce compost, bio-fertilizers, compressed biogas, refuse-derived fuel (RDF), and recoverable plastics, metals, and paper.

E-Waste: Urban Mining at Scale

Karnataka generates around 2.9 lakh tonnes of e-waste annually, valued at nearly ₹2,886 crore. Yet only 10–15% is formally recovered. E-waste contains valuable metals such as gold, silver, copper, and rare earth elements critical for modern technologies. Developing a robust urban mining ecosystem could unlock over ₹2,500 crore annually while reducing dependence on imported raw materials.

Construction and Demolition Waste

Accounting for 15–25% of urban waste, construction and demolition (C&D) waste is another underutilised resource. Processed properly, it can produce M-sand, aggregates, bricks, and paver blocks, reducing dependence on natural sand mining. The market potential for recycled construction material is estimated at ₹2,000–4,000 crore annually.

Sewage and Wastewater

Sewage and wastewater are perhaps the most overlooked resources. Treated wastewater can be reused in industry, construction, and landscaping, while sewage sludge can be converted into biogas and electricity. Investment in this segment could transform a liability into a strategic asset.

The Way Forward

The path forward requires a decisive shift from waste management to resource management. Strengthening segregation at source, expanding recycling infrastructure, creating markets for secondary materials, and integrating the informal sector are all critical priorities.

With its strong industrial base, active startup ecosystem, and progressive urban governance, Karnataka is well positioned to transform its waste challenge into a model circular economy — one that addresses environmental concerns while driving economic growth.

The message is clear: waste is not a liability, but a multi-sector resource waiting to be unlocked.
